9. Key Findings

  • wf2 is the best service scenario at both network-month and key-node (C937) level, achieving 100% CFR at C937 in June with moderate inventory.
  • wf1 is dominated by wf2 on every metric — lower service, higher ME inventory, and similar changeover. There is no dimension where wf1 wins.
  • wf3 and wf4 deliver nearly identical inventory relief, but wf3 is consistently safer on service. wf4 is dominated by wf3.
  • Month End Inventory: reduced-SS scenarios (wf3/wf4) carry 14–28% less end-of-period inventory than current-SS scenarios (wf1/wf2). The highest absolute inventory is at 0386 in all scenarios.
  • wf4 has the lowest absolute inventory (119,703 cases network at sim end), but this comes at a service penalty (82.4% at C937).
  • Changeover burden is halved in reduced-SS scenarios (wf3/wf4: 11 May counts) vs current-SS (wf1/wf2: 22–23 May counts). The dominant type is washout-related at 0386.
  • Lane lead times are under control on average, but tail risk (P90 > cfg total LT) persists on C816→C937 in wf1 and wf3.

10. Recommendations

  1. If the decision priority is service protection, choose wf2. It dominates wf1 and has the cleanest C937 service profile.
  2. If the decision priority is balanced inventory relief, choose wf3. It offers −31% ME inventory at 0386, and half the changeover burden, at a moderate service trade-off.
  3. Do not select wf1. wf2 is strictly better on all measured KPIs.
  4. Do not select wf4 over wf3. wf4 has slightly lower absolute inventory but worse service, especially at C937 (82.4% vs 85.0%).
  5. Monitor C816→C937 lane execution risk using the P90 total LT and P90 waiting MOQ from the workbook if either wf1 or wf3 is selected.
