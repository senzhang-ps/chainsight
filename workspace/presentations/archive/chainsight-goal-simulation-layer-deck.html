Design Principle

from source document
🎯

Goal-driven, not model-driven

The system starts from business questions and desired outcomes, not from whatever model happens to exist.

🗺️

Capability map, not universal simulator

We do not build one giant simulation engine. We build a map of simulation capabilities with different scope, resolution, maturity, and runtime cost.

📊

Quantification, evaluation, selection

The Goal Simulation Layer does not operate the business. It evaluates possible goals, policies, and strategic choices, then selects or recommends the preferred one.

🔍

Explicit assumptions and confidence

Every result must carry its assumptions, boundary conditions, and verdict quality.

👤

Human-in-the-loop for semantic trade-offs

When business trade-offs cannot be meaningfully reduced to formulas or weights, the system asks for human judgment.

🧩

Requisite variety with bounded complexity

The system must be diverse enough to match supply chain complexity, but its engineering primitives must remain few and controlled.

Final Goal Output

Goal Expression — Weight Illusion vs Business-Native Semantics

✗

Weight Illusion

A black-box numeric score that looks precise but is hard to defend.

1
Arbitrary numerical values
0.37 / 0.42 / 0.21 are rarely business-grounded.
2
Lack real-world business semantic
Numbers do not explain cash floor, service threshold, or risk appetite.
3
Difficult to justify or interpret
Stakeholders cannot audit why one future is preferred.
✓

Business-Native Expression

Semantic and explicit: a decision contract stakeholders can inspect.

🔒
Hard constraints
Cash cannot fall below X. OTIF cannot fall below Y.
📏
Satisficing thresholds
Above 96% service, additional improvement has diminishing value.
🥇
Priority order
In survival mode, protect cash first, then service, then cost.
🎲
Risk appetite
Avoid strategies with unacceptable P95 downside.
❓
Trade-off questions
Is 1% higher service worth 3M additional cash tied in inventory?
👤
HITL
When the trade-off is strategic or ambiguous, ask the business owner.

Line M/Y/C Sourcing Network — Supply Choice & Safety Stock What-If

Goal

Evaluate four what-if scenarios that combine supply choice policy and safety stock policy across the full sourcing network for Line M/Y/C SKUs, so the team can compare service and inventory implications before running simulation.

Scope Hypothesis

  • Network scope: the full network for Line M/Y/C sourcing SKUs
  • Simulation period: 2026-05-04 to 2026-06-19
  • Baseline planning version: 50/50 BOP using the 2026-04-27 LBE version
  • Initial inventory basis: 2026-04-27 actual inventory, including stock on hand and in-transit

Constraints & Assumptions

  • High-side logic: maintain delta in supply choice
  • Forecast error CoV: follow iBPI
  • AO: assume 0%
  • Safety stock for what-if 1 and what-if 2: follow iBPI after override
  • Safety stock for what-if 3 and what-if 4: DTC locations reduce from 30 days to 15 days
  • Run command / simulation codebase path: user_not_provided

Baseline

  • Produce policy baseline anchor: 50/50 BOP, Apr 27 version LBE
  • Order policy baseline anchor: current safety stock unless scenario states reduced safety
  • KPI baseline values for service, inventory, and supply stability: TBD

Success Criteria

  • All four confirmed scenarios have aligned scope, assumptions, config targets, and output paths
  • The project is ready for config package generation and simulation run handoff without re-asking the core business setup
  • Comparison KPIs and decision rule for selecting a preferred scenario: TBD

Confirmed Scenarios

  1. What-if 1: produce to high-side, order as 50/50 forecast × current safety
  2. What-if 2: produce to 50/50, order as high-side × current safety
  3. What-if 3: produce to high-side, order as 50/50 forecast × reduced safety

Self-Evolution System + Admin Dashboard

Foundation capabilities from chainsight_intro.html: memory capture, recall, consolidation, proposal approval and admin operations.

Self-Evolution Flow

1

Capture

Tool results → observation / pitfall; user conversation → feedback; explicit agent write → memory_write. Each memory includes structured context and ontology anchors.

2

Store

cognition.db stores episodic → semantic → procedural memories. Types include pitfall, method, observation, feedback and deviation.

3

Recall

Three-path retrieval: exact ref match, ontology one-hop expansion and FTS5 keyword fallback, with salience scoring.

4

Consolidate

Session-end 10-stage pipeline promotes, deduplicates, detects new concepts / metadata, extracts rules, tracks coverage and distills knowledge.

S1 graduateS2 dedupS3 detect_newS4 detect_metaS5 extract_rulesS6 link_sourceS7 deviationS8 track_covS9 skill_learnS10 distill
5

Approve & Apply

Low-risk proposals can be auto-approved. High-risk new_class / new_edge / new_rule proposals require human review before writing JSON-LD / YAML.
