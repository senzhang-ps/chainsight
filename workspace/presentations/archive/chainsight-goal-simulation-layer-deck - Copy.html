Problem We Solve & Design Principles

The Core Question

NOT the right question
Can the agent perform a task?
↓
THE real question
Which goal should the system pursue, why, under what assumptions, and with what confidence?
Key insight: Same reality + different GSL = different system “soul”. Cash-first, service-first and resilience-first agents make different choices from the same data.

Six Design Principles

Goal-Driven

Start from business questions, not available models

Capability Map

Capabilities with scope, maturity, runtime cost

Quantify & Select

Evaluate goals and policies; does not operate business

Explicit Assumptions

Each result carries confidence and boundary

Human-in-Loop

Ask humans for strategic semantic trade-offs

Bounded Complexity

Few controlled primitives, not arbitrary composition

Understand

Goal Interpreter converts intent into objectives, constraints, thresholds, priorities, risk appetite and trade-off questions. Decision Context defines entity, geography, horizon, candidate strategies and acceptable uncertainty.

Route

Capability Router selects certified capability, executable idiom, analytical sandbox or backlog. Capability Map records scope, resolution, runtime, maturity, inputs and validation boundary.

Execute

Scenario Binding binds demand, supply, lead time, capacity, disruption and policy assumptions. Simulation Run executes and records evidence.

Evaluate

Outcome Evaluation creates cost, cash, service, inventory, carbon, resilience and risk vectors. Verdict + HITL labels quality and asks humans for ambiguous calls.

Final Goal Output

Business-Native Goal Expression — Without Weight Illusion

✗ Avoid: Weight Illusion

cost_weight    = 0.37
service_weight = 0.42
cash_weight    = 0.21
Arbitrary numbers hide business meaning, assumptions and human judgment.

✓ Use: Business-Native Expression

🔒 Cash cannot fall below $2M
📏 OTIF must stay ≥ 95%
📊 Priority: cash → service → cost
⚠ Avoid P95 downside above $5M
❔ Is +1% service worth $3M inventory?
👤 Ask owner for strategic calls

Goal Expression Primitives

Hard Constraint

Non-negotiable boundary

Satisficing

Good-enough threshold

Priority Order

Business ranking

Risk Appetite

Tail-risk boundary

Trade-off Q

Explicit value exchange

HITL Trigger

Escalation condition

Final output: selected goal / strategy / policy with explicit assumptions, validity boundary, confidence and HITL rationale.
